Return one JSON object and nothing else, with exactly these keys:

  "speech_act": one of: asks_question | shares_experience | gives_advice | offers_support | shares_information,
  "emotion": [1-2 of: fear | frustration | grief | hope | wellbeing | gratitude | none],
  "care_experience": one of: not_mentioned | positive | dismissed | delayed_diagnosis | access_barrier,
  "subject": one of: self | family_member | general,
  "health_claim": one of: no_claim | personal_experience_claim | general_health_claim | causal_or_cure_claim,

FIELDS

speech_act (asks_question | shares_experience | gives_advice | offers_support | shares_information)
    What the post does. A post that tells a story and also asks the readers something is
    asks_question: getting an answer is the point of writing. Advice aimed at a reader
    outranks background narrative. offers_support is sympathy, encouragement, prayers or
    thanks with no substantive content of its own.

emotion (fear | frustration | grief | hope | wellbeing | gratitude | none)
    Affect the author expresses in this post. It is none UNLESS you can point at a word or
    phrase that states a feeling. Describing a frightening event is not fear ('I had a heart
    attack' is none); saying so is ('I was terrified'). Describing a good outcome is not
    wellbeing ('the test was clear' is none); saying so is ('I feel great'). A phrase that
    PERFORMS the feeling counts even when it does not name it: 'Hang in there' and 'You will
    make it' are hope. The emotion is the AUTHOR'S OWN: another person's improvement is not
    the author's wellbeing, and saying others gave up hope is not the author's despair.
    'Feel reassured' is a worry resolved, so wellbeing rather than hope. Judge what is
    written, never what the events would normally make someone feel. Give a SECOND emotion
    only when it has its own clause; where the second is merely implied by the first, or you
    are unsure whether it applies, give one emotion only. wellbeing = reports feeling well,
    improved, or a worry resolved. gratitude needs the author thanking or appreciating
    someone; offering prayers for someone else is not gratitude. none means no affect at all
    and must appear alone, never beside another value.

care_experience (not_mentioned | positive | dismissed | delayed_diagnosis | access_barrier)
    HOW THE CARE WENT for a specific person whose case the post describes, whoever that is.
    Choose anything other than not_mentioned only when you can point at words showing
    whether the care was good or bad. Three things are not_mentioned: no care described at
    all; clinical events narrated with no sign of how they went ('I had a cath on Tuesday');
    and opinions about doctors in general rather than a described case ('doctors never
    listen to women'). Recommendations, referrals and explanations a clinician gave are also
    not_mentioned on their own: what was advised is not how the advising went. An evaluation
    of one's own care is enough on its own, with no action attached: 'my cardiologist is
    great' is positive. The clinical OUTCOME is not the care experience: 'the surgery was a
    success' is about the body, not about how the clinicians treated anyone. access_barrier
    further requires that the care was NOT OBTAINED: difficulty, delay or expense on the way
    to care that was still received is positive, dismissed or delayed_diagnosis instead.
    positive = clinicians listened, acted promptly, were competent or kind; dismissed =
    symptoms minimised, disbelieved, or put down to anxiety, stress or weight without a
    workup; delayed_diagnosis = the right answer came late, repeat visits or a misdiagnosis
    before it was found; access_barrier = care could not be obtained at all (cost,
    insurance, waiting lists, distance, no specialist). A general opinion with no episode
    behind it (doctors never listen to women) is not_mentioned, and so is a dismissal the
    author only feared or imagined. If several apply: dismissed > delayed_diagnosis >
    access_barrier > positive.

subject (self | family_member | general)
    Whose health the post is mainly about. self = the author's own; family_member = a named
    or identified person close to them; general = no specific individual, or someone the
    author only addresses without describing their case. This is about whose health, not
    whose feelings: my husband had a heart attack and I am terrified is family_member with
    emotion fear. Advice to a reader that also mentions the author's own case is self only
    where their own health is described substantively; a passing mention in support of the
    advice stays general.

health_claim (no_claim | personal_experience_claim | general_health_claim | causal_or_cure_claim)
    The strongest generalisable health proposition present in the post, whether the author
    asserts it, questions it or quotes someone else. no_claim = none;
    personal_experience_claim = a statement about the author alone (magnesium stopped my
    palpitations); general_health_claim = a statement presented as holding for other people;
    causal_or_cure_claim = a generalised cause, cure, prevention or reversal. no_claim vs
    personal_experience_claim: bare history (I had a stent in 2019) is no_claim, and becomes
    a claim only where the post links something to an effect (I had a stent and the pain
    went). This field does not judge whether the claim is true: correct claims are labelled
    the same way. Strongest scope wins.

womens_specific (true | false)
    true when the content concerns heart health in ways specific to women: pregnancy or
    postpartum cardiac problems, menopause, HRT or oestrogen and cardiac risk, SCAD,
    Takotsubo, sex differences in symptoms, diagnosis or treatment, or dismissal attributed
    to being a woman. The author being a woman does not by itself make the content women-
    specific.

Rules that override any instinct to the contrary:
  1. Label only the post given to you. For a reply you also get the opening of
     the parent post: use it to resolve what the reply refers to, but annotate
     the reply.
  2. Label what is written, not what you infer. A post that recounts a severe
     event in a flat, factual register has emotion "none", however frightening
     the event was. Do not supply feelings the author did not express.
  3. Placeholders [URL] [EMAIL] [EMAIL:ORG] [PHONE] [USER] [NAME] [ADDRESS]
     [ID] [DOB] mark spans that were redacted. They never change a label: read
     "[NAME] told me" as "a person told me".
  4. No medical knowledge is needed. In particular health_claim records whether
     a generalisable claim is present, never whether it is true.
  5. Every field is independent except where a priority rule says otherwise.
  6. Choose only from the listed values, and always commit to one.
